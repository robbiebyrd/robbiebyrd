I'm a Full Stack Software Engineer, specializing in developing systems at scale. Currently, I'm a Senior Software Engineer at Realtor.com.

## About me
Me and my husband, Josh, moved to Austin in 2012 from Mobile, Alabama, where we had both lived for several years. Before that, I lived in many small towns throughout the Southeast. Previously a journalist, I am a code autodidact polyglot.

### I am...
A Full Stack engineer, writing web applications, designing user interfaces, and creating the software and infrastructure to make them run resiliently and without intervention (or at least try to).

I write software in Go(lang), Python and Typescript primarily, but have experience in others. I enjoy working both on Back- and Front-End projects.

## What I did
I did not start out to be a software engineer; in college, I split my major between Information Technology and Journalism. After three years of college, I was offered a position as the News Editor of my hometown newspaper. I covered everything from gardening club meetings to executions, eventually specializing in crimes, courts and capital punishment (I have had my articles used in more legal briefs than I can name). I've worked at 7 different newspapers throughout the Southeast before moving full time into software engineering.

I taught myself ColdFusion and was hired by an advertising agency, where I made banner ads, websites and phone apps for clients large and small. I spent another few years in journalism, building web applications for hundreds of local TV stations throughout the company.

I successively improved my development skills, and since have worked developing smart home hub software, web hosting platforms, retail point-of-sale applications and software for the US Government.

For a brief time, I was also a DJ and a theatrical technical director.

Mental calm and a good dog park. Until then, I enjoy playing with new technologies for the sake of learning.

### I’ve discovered that I best like working in the following ways...
* I enjoy broad direction with clearly-stated, well-defined goals and a list of restraints (such as deadlines and budgets).
* Because I've never worked on a project where some part of the direction didn't change, I prefer to break the work up into smaller, interchangeable and interconnectable, so that changes in the direction don't affect the whole project.
* I prefer consistent communication; the biggest trap one can fall into is taking silence for continued acceptance.

### My absolute requirements to do great work are ...
A lot of RAM, local administrator access, decent WiFi and a hot beverage.
